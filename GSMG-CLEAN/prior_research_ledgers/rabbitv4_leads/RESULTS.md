# Lead workstreams: 12 Sep 2026 results

All tests were offline, and gsmg.io was never contacted. The targets are the three locked envelopes: terminal `b45a5e3d827593ca`, SalPhaseIon `3ab585348552415d` and Cosmic `2d3f6fe06dc950e6`.

**How a key is accepted.** The final ciphertext block is decrypted using the previous block as the chaining value, so a correct key is recognised even if the IV derivation differs. A result is a hit if the plaintext is 64 hex characters followed by a full `0x10` block, or if it is more than 90% printable.

**Controls** (`python harness.py`):
- A synthetic 64-hex plaintext is detected correctly.
- The real Phase 3.2 outer envelope is accepted with its verified password.

**Password forms.** Unless noted otherwise, each input was tried as `sha256hex` and as raw text, under EVP-SHA256 (and EVP-MD5 where marked).

## Facts established along the way

- **The poster uses the Windows 7 MS Paint default palette**: Indigo (63,72,204), Yellow (255,242,0) and Red (237,28,36). The only red is a full-width 15 px stripe directly under the 14×14 grid, which acts as a 15th row.
- **The Paint palette gives each colour a position.** Red is column 4 and Rose sits under it in the white row. That is one literal reading of "Roses are White but often Red / Yellow has a number (col 6) and so does Blue (col 9)". It is not tested beyond the sum-list models in C.
- **661 is the 121st prime.** Jerry's 121-character selection is therefore exact. S121 and F91 are saved in `out/d_streams.json`.
- **ArchOptic's "absvolt decrypts the terminal blob" (#10597) is a one-byte `0x01` padding false positive**, which a wrong key produces about 1 time in 256.
- **Diego's 103 digits are the A1Z26 digits of the 64-letter YOUWON tail** of the DBBI−VIC candidate. Their "enter = 13224" is an Executive Order number, as is the Phase 2 answer 11110 (JFK's EO 11110). This is noted, not established as meaningful.
- **Every 11th letter of FAED ends in 13224, which is chance.** `FAED[10::11]` mapped a=1…i=9 is `347693177767219789578792418994213878532263772613224`. Across all skip samples of FAED (steps 1–60, every offset) `13224` occurs once; a random 5-digit number occurs 0.45 times on average. "Ends with" has P ≈ 0.03 per pattern, and that framing was chosen after seeing it among 21 derived streams. The string opens no envelope and is not the prize key.
- **Context of the passport hint.** #8048 answers Lazy Prophet's joke questions (#8045: "how old are you? are you married? what is the pk of the puzzle address?"). It reads as a refusal to give a birth date, not a pointer into the puzzle. The creator then called Diego's passport ↔ EO 13224 link a coincidence (#70307).
- **DBBI begins `dbbi`, and EO 13224 cites "1(b), 1(c), 1(d)(i)".** Both use the same a–i letter set. Selecting, deleting or zeroing those letters produces nothing: no 64-hex, no English, no key.
- **YOUWON in DBBI−VIC is not chance** (P ≈ 3.2×10⁻⁶ from DBBI's letter frequencies; the 91-letter lengths leave one alignment). Its 64-letter tail carries no freedom beyond DBBI's own digits, so the 9 forced marker letters (V, I, C, YOUWON) are the natural "zeroed out" candidates. Details are in `ARCHITECT_DIFF_AND_FIRST_HINT.md`.
- **"One for one, four for one"** (inserted into the Phase 3.2 intro) matches the 1/4 straddles of the checkerboard that decodes the VIC text. It shows that an inserted Architect sentence can carry a key parameter.
- **The puzzle's Architect text removes every "choice" line and the doors passage.** `lastwordsbeforearchichoice` must therefore point into the film scene, or to the puzzle's "select", not to a choice inside the decoded text.
- **DBBI and FAED are not the sibling decimal→ASCII encoding with some digits zeroed or removed.** The search in H is exhaustive, not sampled. Both controls are recovered exactly. Chance solutions would be expected about 2^-24 (DBBI) to 2^-200 (FAED) times, and none exist. So whatever "zeroed out" means, it does not apply to these blocks under that decode. It would have to act after some other transform (e.g. the DBBI−VIC step) or use a different number-to-text reading.
- **Mixing the poster colours cannot reveal hidden text: every blue or yellow cell is the last (LSB) bit of one URL character.** The 24 markers sit at spiral bits 7, 15, …, 191, and blue = 1, yellow = 0 there (already noted in `connection_audit_2026_09_10.txt`). Recolouring them only shifts letters by ±1 (e.g. `gsmg/io/uieseeeisqmaouee`), and every other colour→bit map or 14×15 red-row spiral gives non-text. "Yellow has a number and so does Blue" therefore literally states the first piece's encoding (yellow = 0, blue = 1 on the character boundary). Read that way, "go back to the first puzzle piece" points to its method (spiral binary, with colour marking each 8th bit), not to more data inside the poster.
- **No later a–i text has the poster's structure.** In the poster, one class of symbols (blue/yellow) sits on exactly one bit position in 8 and appears nowhere else, so the text marks its own byte boundaries. Across every grid shape and reading order, the best such class covers 25% of positions in DBBI, 27% in F91 and 0% in S121 and FAED. That is below letter-shuffled copies (maximum 40%, 30%, 20%). The binary decode is also chance-level. The best real streams score 12/13 bytes in `[a-z0-9./]` for DBBI and F91 (e.g. `_i/7uvnaa2jr1`), and 17/20 of the DBBI shuffles do as well. F91 shuffles reach 13/13, and S121 shuffles score as high as the real S121 or higher (0.94 vs 0.82). None of these texts is a two-class binary image under the first piece's encoding.
- **The "very specific" hint (#8310/#8311/#8315/#8328) is the *Cosmic Duality* cover** (Time-Life *Voyage Through the Universe*): a purple and orange-gold starfield yin-yang. Together with purple pill (#17891) and purple→orange carrots (#49176), the creator's colour remarks all point at that yin-yang.
- **The Wayback archive of gsmg.io hides no route.** The CDX listing (411 gsmg.io and 618 subdomain rows) was checked, along with the Vue bundles from 2019, 2022, 2025 and 2026 and the 2020 `app.js.map`. The router lists only public pages (`/puzzle`, `/register`, `/risk`, `/yummy`, …), and no bundle contains a 64-hex string or a phase name, so the phase pages were served by the server. The 11 archived 64-hex paths beyond the known ones are all 12.2–12.3 KB, which is the app's catch-all shell, and none matches a hash in I. Until mid-2026 every unknown path returned that 200 shell. The 9-byte `Hello :-)` 404 only appears after the July 2026 rebuild, so the live site now answers only for pages carried over to the new host.
- **gsmg.io was a Laravel app with accounts in 2021** (`/api/v1/subscriptions`, `/register?referral=`, `/dashboard`). So when #8000 says "we can't check that" (Dec 2021), it cannot have meant that visits to a page were invisible, because server logs would show them. It fits a door that is opened entirely offline (a decryption or a key) at least as well as a URL.
- **Looking Forward contains no "architect", "matrix", "rabbit" or "in front of your eyes".** "Bingo" confirms the book as the recommended reading. It does not make the book the literal source of that phrase.
